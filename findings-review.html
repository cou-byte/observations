Beyond the headlines

The value is in the explanation.

Some challenges sound familiar. The Listening Tour's contribution will depend on what it reveals about how those challenges arise, whose experiences differ, when data use succeeds, and what conditions support improvement.

Why does it happen?

Explore the workflows, handoffs, access constraints, and organizational conditions behind a reported experience.

When does it work well — and for whom?

Compare successful and difficult cases rather than assuming that a common challenge affects every group in the same way.

What could make a difference?

Identify improvements participants prioritize, distinguish common ground from tradeoffs, and test recommendations against the evidence.

Research status and next steps

Listening first. Analyzing carefully.

The Listening Tour draws on interviews, focus groups, strategic consultations, and a statewide survey. Emerging observations are starting points for deeper analysis — not substitutes for it.

Examine experiences

Review specific successful and difficult cases, including the work required to find, prepare, interpret, and use information.

Compare perspectives

Look for patterns, variations, exceptions, and differences across the groups participating in the tour.

Review before release

Confirm quotations, consent and privacy requirements, research interpretations, and the appropriate publication process before public dissemination.

One question.

What happens between collecting education data and using it to support a student? Follow one hypothetical situation—then experience how the same moment looks different depending on where you stand.

Explore the story ↗

A design exploration, not a summary of what Listening Tour participants said.

One data point → three perspectives → three questions
The Listening Tour explores how people use education data, what works, and what gets in the way.
Start with a situation

A school is trying to spot who needs help.

Imagine a school reviewing student literacy information to decide where additional support might be useful. The information exists—but before it can help, people must collect it, interpret it, and decide what to do. The same process looks different to a teacher, a district data specialist, and a family.

This example is fictional. It is designed to test a storytelling interface. It does not represent a real participant, interview, quotation, documented pattern, or recommendation from the 2026–27 Listening Tour.
01 / Follow the journey

Before data can help, it has to move.

Move through three moments. Each one illustrates a question our research may help us understand—not an answer we've already found.

Choose a moment
Moment 01 / Collect

The numbers are available. Are they easy to bring together?

A team might look at assessment results, attendance, and classroom observations. These pieces of information can live in different places and arrive on different schedules.

Question to explore

What does it take for the right people to access the information they need?

02 / Change your point of view

Same situation.
Different needs.

Choose a perspective to explore what someone in that role might need from the information. These are hypothetical user journeys, not testimony or empirical comparisons.

03 / Imagine another path

What would make this easier?

A research experience should help visitors move from an example to a productive question. We can explore possibilities without presenting them as findings or recommendations.

Design thought experiment

What if information were easier to locate, understand, and discuss across roles?

Possible alternative—not a recommendation

Imagine a shared view that clearly identifies the source, timing, and meaning of each data point, while giving educators and families a way to ask questions. Would this make the decision more useful? What new concerns might it create?

04 / Follow the evidence

The story opens the door. The research must support what follows.

In the finished experience, visitors will be able to move from a real, approved story to its research context, relevant findings, differences across perspectives, and important limitations.

Our publishing rule: No illustrative scene, example, or model response may be mistaken for a Listening Tour finding. Actual evidence appears only after review and the necessary permissions.
